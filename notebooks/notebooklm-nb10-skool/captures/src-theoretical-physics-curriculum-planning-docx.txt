Classical Mechanics
The Impetus Theory: The belief that an object in motion contains a quantity of "force" that is gradually used up, causing it to stop.
Newton's First Law: An object maintains constant velocity unless acted upon by a net external force; motion does not require a continuous force.
Thermodynamics
Thermal Equivalency: Treating heat and temperature as identical quantities, or assuming metals are inherently colder than wood.
Heat is the transfer of thermal energy; temperature is average kinetic energy; materials possess different thermal conductivities.
Electromagnetism
The Current Consumption Model: The notion that electric current is "used up" by components (like lightbulbs) in a circuit.
Charge is conserved; energy is transferred and dissipated by components, but the current itself remains constant in a series circuit.
Optics
Extramission Theory: The belief that human vision is enabled by rays or energy emanating outward from the eye to the object.
Vision occurs when light rays reflect off an object and travel into the eye, stimulating the retina.
